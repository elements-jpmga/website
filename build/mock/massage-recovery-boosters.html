A little more, where you need it

Boosters are short, focused add-ons you pair with any Massage & Recovery treatment. Choose suction from cupping, stone gua sha or buffalo-horn bojin, a warm herbal volcanic mud mask, infrared moxa warmth, or deeper muscle blade work — each brings a different kind of help to the same tired areas.

How boosters work

A booster adds 15 minutes to the treatment you book, so your massage time stays whole. You choose the booster when you book, and your therapist works it into your session on the areas you want focused.

Boosters go with any Massage & Recovery treatment — a 60-minute massage, a 30-minute therapy such as Fascia Release or Medi-Stretch®, or a women's wellness treatment.

Cupping

Cupping is for deep muscle tension that hands alone don't fully shift — the stuck, heavy kind that sits across the upper back and shoulders.

How it works

Warm glass cups create a gentle vacuum that holds them to the skin. Instead of pressing down, the suction lifts the skin and the fascia beneath it, easing tension from a different direction. Your therapist may leave the cups in place or use gliding cupping — moving the cup across oiled skin while the suction holds — to work along a wider area. In TCM, this movement is used to encourage the flow of qi and clear stagnation.

What you may feel

A pulling, drawing sensation rather than pressure. Afterwards, tight muscles often feel looser and lighter, and the massage that follows can feel deeper.

Suits you if you have

  • Back or shoulder tension that feels stuck, however often you stretch
  • Aches and fatigue after a long week or hard training
  • A preference for pressure lifted out rather than pressed in
02   /   +15 minutes

Guasha

Gua sha is for tight, tense areas and the sluggish, heavy feeling that builds along the neck, shoulders and back.

How it works

Gua sha is a traditional Chinese tool technique. A smooth, flat tool — usually tourmaline or jade — is glided firmly over oiled skin along the meridian lines. The repeated strokes work through tight fascia and support circulation and lymphatic flow in the area. In TCM terms, the strokes help release stagnant qi.

What you may feel

A firm, smooth scraping glide, with warmth building in the area as you go. Tension and tight fascia feel released, and the body feels lighter after fatigue, stress or overuse. With regular sessions, you may also notice skin in the treated area looking more toned.

Suits you if you have

  • A stiff neck and shoulders from long hours at a desk
  • A run-down, heavy feeling from stress or overuse
  • Tightness that returns soon after a massage

Bojin

Bojin is for deep-seated tension and stubborn knots along the neck, shoulders, back and joints.

How it works

Bojin is a traditional Chinese technique using a buffalo horn tool. Your therapist uses precise pressing, scraping and kneading on acupressure points and along the meridians, working into the deeper layers of muscle, tendon and fascia. Where gua sha uses broad, gliding strokes, bojin is more targeted. In TCM terms, it helps release meridian blockages and support the flow of qi and circulation.

What you may feel

Firm, precise pressure on each knot, followed by a lighter, more relaxed feeling. Stiffness in the neck, shoulders and back eases, and moving feels easier.

Suits you if you have

  • Deep tension that massage alone doesn't fully reach
  • Stiffness in the neck, shoulders, back or joints
  • Tender knots at specific points

Volcanic Mud Mask

The Volcanic Mud Mask is for tired, aching muscles and joints, and a heavy, puffy feeling in the body.

How it works

Herbal extracts are blended into mineral-rich volcanic mud, warmed and applied directly to the skin. Your therapist places it over the area you want worked on — back, spine, tummy, knees or other joints. The warmth helps the herbal blend absorb into the skin, while the mud helps draw out impurities. In TCM, the warmth is also used to expel wind, cold and damp from the body.

What you may feel

A gentle tingling as the warm mud settles over tired muscles. Afterwards, muscles feel soothed and less fatigued.

Suits you if you have

  • Muscle and joint aches in the back, knees or other joints
  • Water retention, or a puffy, heavy feeling
  • Bloating, constipation or period-related discomfort

Infra Ray Moxibustion

Infra Ray Moxibustion is for cold hands and feet, stiffness and fatigue that feel worse when you're cold.

How it works

Moxibustion is a traditional Chinese practice of warming the body with burning dried mugwort (moxa). Here, infrared rays replace the burning herb, delivering deep, targeted warmth to acupuncture points or the area you want worked on — smoke-free, with steady, consistent heat. In TCM, this warmth is used to support qi flow and clear the cold and damp linked to sluggishness and swelling.

What you may feel

An even warmth spreading inwards, easing stiffness, backache, muscle tension and period-related discomfort.

Suits you if you have

  • Cold hands and feet, or stiffness that worsens in air-conditioning
  • Fatigue and sluggishness that feel worse in the cold
  • Period-related discomfort
06   /   +15 minutes

Muscle Blade

Muscle Blade is for stubborn knots and tight fascia that keep returning.

How it works

A warm, specially shaped tool scrapes and glides over muscles and trigger points. It is similar to gua sha but works with firmer pressure on deeper muscle layers, helping break down tough knots and muscle adhesions and loosen tight fascia.

What you may feel

Firm, focused pressure on each knot, and muscles that move more freely afterwards — easing the discomfort that builds from stress, posture or overuse.

Suits you if you have

  • Knots that keep returning from posture, stress or overuse
  • Tight fascia that limits how freely you move
  • A preference for firmer, deeper work than massage alone

Good to know

Your questions
answered

What is a booster?

A booster is a 15-minute add-on that brings a specific technique — suction, tool work, warm mud or infrared warmth — to the areas you want focused. It is added to your booked treatment, so your massage time is not shortened.

Which treatments can I add a booster to?

Any Massage & Recovery treatment, from a 60-minute massage to a 30-minute therapy. Boosters are not offered with Spa Rituals.

How do I choose a booster?

Start with how your body feels — the table above matches common feelings to each booster. Tell us what you'd like to focus on when you book, and we'll talk you through the options so you can decide. Mention your choice when you book on WhatsApp.

Will cupping or gua sha leave marks?

They can. Both may leave temporary redness or round marks on the skin that usually fade within a few days. If you have an event coming up, mention it when you book.

What is moxibustion?

Moxibustion is a traditional Chinese practice that warms acupuncture points by burning dried mugwort (moxa) close to the skin. Our Infra-Ray Moxa uses infrared rays to deliver that warmth instead — smoke-free, with steady heat on the points or area you want worked on.

How is Antedote Aromatherapy different from a booster?

Aromatherapy is used within your booked massage time, so it adds no minutes — only the S$28 blend charge. A booster adds 15 minutes of a separate technique.

When does the S$44 first-trial price apply?

When you add a booster to a first-trial massage. See the first-trial terms.

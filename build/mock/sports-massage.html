About the treatment

Focused on the muscles you use

Sports massage is a firm, targeted massage that concentrates on the muscle groups you use most — whether from running, lifting or long hours at a desk. Each session combines deep tissue work, trigger point therapy, stretching and Swedish massage techniques. It suits every body type and fitness level, not only athletes.

Sports massage works where your body takes the most strain from physical activity. The pressure is firm and the work is concentrated on specific muscle groups — the calves, hamstrings and hips of a runner, the shoulders and back of someone who lifts, or the neck and upper back of someone who types all day. Rather than spreading the time evenly across the body, the session goes deeper into the areas that feel tight, sore or restricted.

Not only for athletes

You don't need to compete to benefit. Sports massage suits anyone with muscle tension, aches or imbalances, or stiffness around the joints — whether it comes from exercise, everyday life or work. Office workers with neck and shoulder strain, people with physically repetitive jobs and weekend runners all come to it for the same reason: muscles that feel tight and need focused attention.

A little more clarity

Your questions answered

Is sports massage only for athletes?

No. Sports massage suits all body types and fitness levels. It helps anyone with muscle tension, aches, imbalances or stiffness around the joints — whether that comes from exercise, your lifestyle or your work.

How is sports massage different from Deep Tissue Massage?

They share some techniques but have different focuses. Sports massage concentrates on the specific muscle groups you use in your activities, combining deep tissue work, stretching and trigger point therapy to support recovery and flexibility. Deep Tissue Massage looks after general muscle health, using slow, firm strokes to work through knots, adhesions and stiffness in the deeper layers — well suited to long-standing tension. If your tightness comes from training or a repetitive activity, start with sports massage; if it's deep-set tension without a clear cause, Deep Tissue Massage may suit you better.

How often should I have a sports massage?

For a specific concern: 5 sessions over 3 weeks.

For maintenance: once or twice a month.

Is sports massage painful?

It can feel intense, but it should never be unbearable. Some techniques can cause mild discomfort, especially when your muscles are very tight or have knots. Let your therapist know at any time and the pressure will be adjusted.

Can sports massage help with lower back pain when I run?

Yes — many of our clients come to us with exactly this. Sports massage eases tension in the lower back, hips and glutes, improves flexibility and blood flow, and works on the muscle imbalances that often contribute to lower back discomfort while running. Regular sessions help keep that tightness from building up again.

Can sports massage help with old injuries?

Yes — it helps loosen stiff scar tissue and ease tightness around areas that have been strained before.

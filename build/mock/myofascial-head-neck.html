About This Treatment

Myofascial Head / Neck Therapy — 头部疗法 (tóu bù liáo fǎ) in Chinese — is focused work on the fascia of your scalp and neck. Your therapist uses hand massage and gua sha to release connective tissue that has tightened, and applies Red Ginger Elixir to your neck.

When fascia tightens

Fascia is the network of connective tissue that wraps around your muscles and organs. Healthy fascia is elastic and flexible, so the muscles it wraps can move freely. When it stiffens, it can cause pain, discomfort and restricted movement.

In the head and neck, this tends to show up as a tight band around your scalp, a stiff neck that is hard to turn, or tension that builds through a long day at the screen.

Why the neck and scalp are treated together

Your neck and scalp are joined by the same continuous sheet of tissue, so tension in one travels to the other. A knot in your neck can pull on your scalp, and a tight scalp pulls on your forehead and around your eyes.

Working on the neck loosens the anchor point at the bottom. Gua sha on the scalp loosens the tension at the top. Treating both lets the whole sheet relax, rather than leaving one end tight.

Tension headaches

Tightness in your scalp and neck can contribute to a tension headache — a feeling like a tight band pressing around your head. Releasing this connected tension helps relax the muscles and ease the discomfort.

Better blood flow

Gua sha and massage encourage blood flow just beneath the skin of your scalp and neck. Releasing tightness at both ends supports this circulation, which can help reduce puffiness, relax the facial muscles and ease stress.

Gua sha, starting gently

Gua sha is a traditional Chinese technique that uses a smooth-edged tool, pressed and stroked along the skin. Because the scalp is sensitive, your therapist starts gently, giving the skin and fascia time to warm up.

Red Ginger Elixir

Red Ginger Elixir is applied to your neck, not your scalp, to boost the effects of the gua sha work. Ginger is used to relieve tight muscles and fascia for three reasons: its anti-inflammatory compounds, its warming effect on circulation and its ability to modulate pain pathways.

Your questions answered

Does it hurt?

A very tight scalp can feel more sensitive, which is why the gua sha starts gently. You are in control of the pressure — ask your therapist to go lighter at any point.

Will gua sha leave marks on my neck?

Some people notice redness afterwards, which fades on its own. If that is a concern, let your therapist know before you start.

Will the elixir leave my hair oily? Can I go straight back to work?

The elixir goes on your neck, not your scalp. If you are heading straight back to work, let your therapist know so they can take extra care around your hair.

How is it different from a head massage?

A head massage focuses mostly on the muscles and skin of your head, mainly for relaxation. This treatment works on your neck and scalp together as one connected system, to address the source of the tension. Gua sha releases tight fascia, while massage works more on the muscles. This treatment uses both.

Can it help with tension headaches?

It helps ease the tension and discomfort linked to tension headaches, by relaxing the connected tissue of your scalp and neck. It is a tension-relief treatment, not a medical treatment for headache disorders.

Will it help me sleep?

It may. The treatment relaxes the scalp and neck muscles and promotes deep relaxation, which can make it easier to switch off and sleep.

How often should I come?

For maintenance: once or twice a month.

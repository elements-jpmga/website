About the treatment

Why long-held tension needs deeper work

Deep tissue massage uses slow, sustained pressure to work into the deeper layers of muscle and the connective tissue around them. It is for the tension that builds over months in the neck, shoulders and back, and that a lighter massage tends to skim over. Your therapist works firmly, within a pressure you can tolerate.

Tension that has built up over months rarely lets go in a single gentle pass. When muscle fibres stay contracted for long stretches (from desk work, screen time, training or stress), the fascia around them can stiffen and the layers of tissue may begin to stick together. The body often compensates by shifting the workload to neighbouring areas. That is why a tight lower back can turn into tight hips or sore shoulders.

A muscle “knot” is a tight, tender area, rather than a literal knot in the fibres. Slow, sustained pressure works on these areas to help ease tension. Myofascial release works along the surrounding connective tissue, called fascia, to help relieve tightness and support easier movement.

Deep tissue massage moves through the surface layers with slow, deliberate strokes to reach those deeper structures. Four techniques do most of the work:

01 / FOCUSED TECHNIQUE

Sustained pressure

firm, held pressure at the points where fibres have knotted. It gives the tissue time to soften and release rather than brace against you.

02 / FOCUSED TECHNIQUE

Cross-fibre friction

strokes across the direction of the muscle fibres, worked into areas where tissue has become stuck, to help it move more freely.

03 / FOCUSED TECHNIQUE

Myofascial release

slow, lengthening pressure along the fascia, the thin connective sheath around each muscle. It eases stiffness and supports easier movement.

04 / FOCUSED TECHNIQUE

Trigger point work

concentrated pressure on tender, knotted spots in a muscle that can send discomfort to other areas or limit how freely you move.

Used together, they work on the knot itself, the tissue around it and the fascia that links it to the next area. That is why deep tissue work can reach tension that lighter massage leaves behind.

Your questions answered

Will deep tissue massage hurt?

Expect firm pressure, and some discomfort where muscles are tight, most noticeable at the moment a knot releases. It should stay within what you can tolerate, and you are in control of that. Tell your therapist if the pressure is too much or not enough, and they will adjust it throughout the session.

What will I feel afterwards?

Some clients notice mild soreness in the treated areas for 24–48 hours, a normal response as the tissue adjusts after deep pressure work. Many also feel looser and move more freely within the same window. Drinking water after your session and avoiding intense exercise for the rest of the day gives the tissue time to recover.

How often should I book?

For a specific concern: 5 sessions over 3 weeks. For maintenance: once or twice a month. A course gives the tissue time to change progressively rather than returning to the same pattern between visits, and you do not need to commit to a course in advance. Your therapist can talk you through what suits you after your first session.

How is it different from a lighter, relaxation massage?

A lighter massage works mainly on the muscle nearer the surface. Deep tissue is slower and firmer, using sustained pressure to reach the deeper muscle layers and the connective tissue around them, which is where long-held tension and knots tend to sit. If you mainly want to unwind, a lighter massage may suit you better. If tension keeps returning to the same places, deep tissue works on it directly.

What is myofascial release?

Fascia is the thin sheet of connective tissue that wraps around every muscle, from individual fibres to whole muscle groups. When it stiffens, movement can feel restricted. Myofascial release is slow, sustained pressure that lengthens along the fascia, easing that stiffness so the muscle can move more freely.

What is a trigger point?

A trigger point is a small, tender knot in a muscle, often felt as a sore spot when pressed. Trigger points can send discomfort to other areas, which is why a tight spot in the shoulder can show up as tension in the neck or head. In your session, concentrated pressure is applied to these spots to help them release.

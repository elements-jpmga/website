About the treatment

Microcurrents at three frequencies

Microcurrents are very low-level electrical currents — gentle enough to work with the body's own electrical signals rather than override them. Acu-Wave uses three frequencies to stimulate nerves and set muscles gently vibrating. In TCM practice, applying them along the meridian channels is thought to strengthen their effect.

The aim is to support your body's natural healing by restoring the electrical signalling that strain, poor health or daily habits can weaken. Each frequency has its own role, and the three are designed to work together.

01

Circulation frequency — getting qi and blood moving

This frequency works on five major acupoints along your meridians to activate and balance the flow of qi. Good circulation carries oxygen and nutrients to your cells and helps clear waste from tired, overworked tissue — which is why this is where the session's energising effect begins.

02

Restoration frequency — supporting your body's own repair

This frequency is designed to help ease inflammation and support the recovery of strained tissue. Research suggests microcurrent may raise levels of ATP — the molecule your cells use for energy — which cells rely on to carry out repair. Microcurrent may also stimulate collagen and elastin, the proteins that keep tissue supple and resilient.

03

Recovery frequency — reaching deep, stubborn knots

This frequency targets trigger points: tight, sensitive spots in a muscle or its surrounding fascia, often called muscle knots. They build up through overuse, strain or injury and cause tension, pain and tenderness — sometimes in a different place altogether. A trigger point in your upper back, for example, can send pain into your shoulder or arm. Microcurrents at this frequency help tight muscle fibres release, so the muscle can finally relax.

Why the three work together

Circulation brings fresh blood flow to the area, Restoration supports the tissue's own recovery, and Recovery releases the knots that keep tension locked in. Working on the flow, the tissue and the knot in one session is what lets Acu-Wave reach aches that keep coming back.

Your questions answered

What is microcurrent therapy?

Microcurrent therapy uses very low-level electrical currents — far gentler than the current in a muscle-stimulation machine. In Acu-Wave, three frequencies are applied at acupoints along your meridians to stimulate nerves, gently vibrate the muscles and support your body's own healing signals.

What does it feel like?

You feel gentle, wave-like vibrations at each pressure point. The treatment is non-invasive.

What is a trigger point?

A trigger point is a tight, sensitive spot in a muscle or its fascia — what most people call a knot. It forms through overuse, strain or injury and can cause pain somewhere else, such as an upper-back knot sending pain into your shoulder or arm. Acu-Wave's Recovery frequency targets these points to help the muscle release.

What are meridians and acupoints?

In TCM, meridians are the pathways through which qi — the body's vital energy — flows, and acupoints are specific points along them. Working on acupoints is how TCM aims to restore balanced flow, and Acu-Wave applies microcurrents at these points rather than across a general area.

How many sessions will I need?

For a specific concern: 12 sessions over 8 weeks. For maintenance: once or twice a month. Some clients notice easier movement or less bloating after their first session, while concerns such as a neck hump tend to change progressively over a course.

Is there any downtime, and what should I do afterwards?

There is no downtime — you can go straight back to your day. Drink plenty of water and rest where you can afterwards.

How should I prepare?

Eat at least two hours before your session, so you are comfortable during the treatment.

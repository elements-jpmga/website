About the treatment

A TCM-inspired treatment that brings meridian therapy and deep-tissue work together. Your therapist uses a buffalo horn Bojin tool and a nourishing ginseng cream to press, knead and scrape along meridian lines and acupressure points — reaching stubborn knots that regular massage may not fully release.

What is Bojin?

Bojin is a Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) technique that applies deep, targeted pressure along the meridians to release deep-seated tension. In TCM, meridians are the pathways through which qi — vital energy — flows, each linked to the body’s organs, so a blockage along them is thought to affect how you feel overall. Pressure on key points is used to release tension and blockages, support circulation and encourage the flow of qi. Many clients find that letting go of physical tension leaves them feeling lighter and calmer, too.

Where there is blockage, there is pain

The treatment follows a core TCM principle: 不通则痛，痛则不通 — “where there is blockage, there is pain; where there is pain, there is blockage.” In this view, tension and stiffness build up where qi and blood stop moving freely. Clearing those blockages lets energy and blood circulate again, helping tight, sluggish muscles soften and recover.

A closer look at your treatment

How the buffalo horn tool works

Your therapist uses a buffalo horn Bojin tool to work on meridian and acupressure points across the body. Its shape and firmness allow precise, concentrated pressure that reaches the deeper layers of muscle, tendon and fascia — without heavy surface bruising. The treatment combines TCM’s understanding of acupoints with Western knowledge of muscle structure:

It targets trigger points and rigid myofascial bands to relieve chronic tightness and restore normal tissue sliding.

The deep-tissue and scraping actions help disrupt and remodel fibrotic scar tissue that forms after a muscle strain or sprain.

The result feels more targeted than a regular massage — firm, focused work on the areas that hold the most tension.

Why ginseng cream

A nourishing ginseng cream is used throughout the session. Ginseng is valued in TCM as a revitalising herb, traditionally used to help ease fatigue and support the flow of qi and blood. Here, it adds a revitalising quality to the deep pressure work.

How Bojin complements massage

Regular massage works broadly, improving overall circulation, energy flow and relaxation. Bojin reaches deeper muscle layers and works through meridian blockages that standard massage may miss. It concentrates on stubborn knots in the shoulders, neck and lower back, for more targeted relief.

Your questions answered

What is Bojin therapy?

Bojin is a TCM technique that uses deep, targeted pressure along the meridians — the pathways that, in TCM, carry qi through the body. Your therapist uses a buffalo horn tool to work on key points, releasing deep-seated tension and blockages and supporting circulation. It reaches deeper than most hands-only massage, which is why it suits stubborn knots.

What techniques are used?

Your therapist uses a buffalo horn tool to apply targeted pressure and scraping along meridian channels and acupressure points, combined with precise pressing and kneading. Together, these help release knots, support the flow of qi and encourage circulation.

Why can Bojin reach deep tissue?

The buffalo horn tool’s shape and hardness concentrate pressure into a small area, so it reaches the deeper layers of muscle, tendon and fascia more precisely than the hands alone. This targets trigger points and rigid myofascial bands, and the deep-tissue and scraping actions help disrupt and remodel fibrotic scar tissue that forms after a muscle strain or sprain. Many clients notice better elasticity, easier movement and deeper relaxation.

Is it painful?

It can be uncomfortable, especially where muscles are very tight — the pressure and scraping are deep by design. Your therapist adjusts the pressure to your comfort throughout, so tell them if you’d like it lighter.

Are there side effects, and what aftercare do you recommend?

You may notice mild redness or tenderness in the treated areas, which usually fades within a day. Drink plenty of water afterwards and avoid strenuous activity for 24 hours.

Who is this therapy suitable for?

It suits anyone with deep muscle tension, stiffness, fatigue or sluggish circulation, and active people who want deeper recovery. It’s also a good fit if you’re drawn to natural, TCM-based wellness.

How often should I come?

For a specific concern: 5 sessions over 3 weeks.

For maintenance: once or twice a month.
